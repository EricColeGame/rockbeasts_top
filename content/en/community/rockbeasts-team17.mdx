### Etiquette: What Helps and What Hurts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Search existing threads before asking | Post the same question in three channels |
| Report bugs with clear reproduction steps | Assume a bug is intentional design |
| Credit other people's discoveries | Repost guides without attribution |
| Give feedback on the game, not the people making it | Attack developers or other players personally |
| Keep spoilers behind warnings | Drop story details in a general chat channel |

Community-sourced information is genuinely valuable here — player experience often surfaces mechanics that patch notes never explain. But it is also uneven. Treat a single forum post as a hypothesis, not a fact, and look for corroboration before you build a strategy around it.

## A Practical Roadmap for New Community Members

If you are starting from zero, a little structure goes a long way. The following sequence works whether you are watching Rockbeasts from its first reveal or arriving after launch.

| Step | Action | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pick two channels, not six | Depth beats scattered attention |
| 2 | Lurk for a week | Learn the tone before you post |
| 3 | Introduce yourself briefly | Makes later questions feel less cold |
| 4 | Ask one specific question | Specific questions get specific answers |
| 5 | Give back once you learn something | Communities run on reciprocity |
| 6 | Keep a personal notes file | You will forget that mechanic by next week |

The single most underrated habit is keeping notes. Band-management games involve a lot of small numbers — relationships, finances, timing windows — and the players who seem effortlessly knowledgeable are usually just the ones who wrote things down.

It also helps to decide what you want from the community. If you want strategy, spend your time in guide threads and wiki talk pages. If you want company, Discord voice channels and stream chats will serve you better. If you want to influence the game, the feedback channels and playtest signups are where your voice actually carries weight. There is no wrong answer, but drifting between all three without committing to any of them is how people end up feeling like outsiders in a community they have technically been part of for months.

Finally, be patient with the developers. Rockbeasts Team17 is the kind of project where a small team is juggling creative ambition, technical constraints, and a fanbase that cares intensely. Constructive feedback moves faster than outrage, every time.
